I'm Stephen Willcock, and PainfulDiodes is my electronics work, done in my spare time: experiments, projects with Arduinos, Raspberry Pis and Picos, and above all designing and building 8-bit computers.

I got into electronics as a child through short-wave radio, and from there to 8-bit computers. My interest in digital electronics was mostly theoretical at first, though I did own a Z80-based Sharp MZ-80B computer and wrote some programs for it in BASIC. Towards the end of my time at school I used a 6502-based Apple II extensively, building hardware add-ons and writing programs in BASIC with some machine code.

My career took me into business software, where I've spent decades driving innovation, designing systems and leading teams; I'm currently working as a Chief Architect. During COVID I came back to electronics through Arduino, and in 2024 I started prototyping a Z80 computer on breadboards, which I retrospectively called BreadZed80.

I transformed that breadboard concept into my first PCB, BeanZee, a Z80 computer board, at the end of 2024, and over the two years since it has grown into a family of boards. BeanZee can still be used standalone, but it can also sit on top of BeanBoard: a keyboard, LCD display and GPIO board. BeanBoardSPI adds a TFT display and flash cartridge storage.

Bean65 is a new 6502 sibling to BeanZee that sits on BeanBoard in the same way, and BeanPort, a USB bridge built around a Raspberry Pi Pico, sits on top of Bean65.

My [blog](https://painfuldiodes.wordpress.com/) is a record of that work as it happens, including the things that went wrong.

Schematics, PCB designs and source code are on [GitHub](https://github.com/PainfulDiodes).

Gareth Halfacree wrote about BeanZee for Hackster: [Stephen Willcock's BeanZee Is a Simple, Compact Zilog Z80 Single-Board Computer with Custom Monitor](https://www.hackster.io/news/stephen-willcock-s-beanzee-is-a-simple-compact-zilog-z80-single-board-computer-with-custom-monitor-1cbe19ed4933).

More and more, what draws me to this is the idea of passing it on. I'd like to help a new generation get the same fascination with electronics and technology that consumed me when I was at school.
